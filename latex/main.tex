\begin{abstract}
Staged binder design (generate a backbone, then inverse-fold a sequence) is thought to stumble at interface
\emph{hotspots}. A prominent report (ProBID-Net) quantifies this as inverse-folding recovery of 0.334 at hotspots
versus 0.472 elsewhere. The phenomenon is real but misread. \textbf{Confidence and competence are two different
derivatives of the same inverse-folding likelihood.} A residue's \emph{confidence} is the \emph{diagonal}, any
scalar of the bound-conditioned distribution $P$, and it measures fold-stability constraint. Its binding
\emph{leverage} is the \emph{mixed second derivative}, the response to ablating the partner. We prove $L$ is not a
function of $P$, so every scalar the field reads off these models is blind to binding \textbf{by construction}.
Empirically, a flexible learner over the entire bound distribution recovers only about 37\% of $L$. The
consequence is large and controlled. On natural complexes (SKEMPI) confidence sits at chance and adds nothing
beyond geometry, while the mixed derivative, a \textbf{zero-shot} readout that uses no binding labels, adds binding
information \textbf{beyond geometry, conservation, and the one-pass log-odds} (the full published feature set, and
it beats a \emph{supervised} baseline), across four inverse-folding architectures. The published deficit is
largely a burial confound. And the direction is \textbf{actionable}. An $+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt on a \emph{frozen}
ProteinMPNN steers it toward higher binding-favorability, confirmed against a matched random control by
independent sequence models, structure predictors, \textbf{and a physics energy function} (FoldX). The physics
function also shows the tilt beats a same-magnitude \emph{confidence} direction, isolating a binding-specific
effect (four judges, two folders, both steering directions, 120 folded complexes). The effect holds when the tilt
is applied on \emph{predicted}, not crystal, backbones, the staged-design regime itself. The leverage operator is
BA-Cycle (Jiao et al., 2024). Our contribution is the decomposition, the identifiability no-go, the first
beyond-geometry-and-conservation control on an inverse-folding binding signal, the feature-class law, and
frozen-model steering. $L$ is the model's \textbf{classifier-free-guidance direction}: to read an un-trained
quantity off a conditional generative model, ablate the conditioner and take the mixed derivative, not the
marginal mistaken for competence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The dominant paradigm for designing protein binders is staged: a generative model proposes a backbone, and an
inverse-folding model assigns a sequence by maximising $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{backbone})$. This
factorisation is convenient, but it omits the very quantity a binder exists to optimise. There is no
binding-energy term anywhere in $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{backbone})$. The concern this raises is sharpest at
\emph{interface hotspots}: the small set of residues that contribute most of the binding free energy, and which
are frequently \emph{frustrated}, buried polar residues or strained rotamers that are locally unfavourable for
the monomer's fold but bought because they pay off in binding. If inverse folding optimises fold-compatibility,
the reasoning goes, it should systematically miss exactly these residues, and a hotspot should sit in the tail of
the model's distribution rather than at its mode. A prominent measurement appears to confirm this: ProBID-Net
reports inverse-folding recovery of 0.334 at hotspots versus 0.472 elsewhere, and attributes the gap to dynamics.

This paper asks one question: when an inverse-folding model is used to build a binder, where, if anywhere, does
its knowledge of \emph{binding} live, and can a designer read it and act on it? The scope is deliberately narrow
and practical: structure-conditioned inverse-folding models (ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF1, and three others),
protein-protein interface binding, and readouts computable at design time. Our answer is that the binding signal
is real, but the field has been reading it from the wrong place. It has read \emph{scalar} summaries of the
model's output (recovery, confidence, a complex-vs-monomer KL) that are blind to binding \textbf{by
construction}, when it lives in a \textbf{mixed second derivative} that is cheap to compute and directly
actionable.

The consequence is immediate for practice, in three forms. As a \textbf{diagnostic}: inverse-folding confidence
is a geometry detector, not a binding score, so a pipeline that distrusts it at the interface (as the field's
leading one-shot designer does, by freezing the interface) is right to, and can now do better. As a
\textbf{training-free ranker}: the mixed derivative locates interface hotspots and adds on top of the exact
feature set published predictors already use, at no training cost. As a \textbf{drop-in steering knob}: a
$+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt biases a \emph{frozen, off-the-shelf} inverse-folding model toward higher-binding
interface residues with no retraining, confirmed by an independent sequence model, an independent structure
predictor, and a physics energy function. The recipe is not protein-specific. The mixed derivative is the model's
\textbf{classifier-free-guidance direction}, so the same move (ablate the conditioner, read the mixed derivative,
not the marginal) applies to any conditional generative model, which is what places the result at a
representation-learning venue rather than a purely methodological one.

We are precise about what is new, because the nearest prior methods already use this operator. The leverage
operator itself, a partner-ablated second forward pass, is the BA-Cycle operator of Jiao et al. (2024), and
decoders such as RedNet already tilt along this direction. Our contribution is not the operator but what it means
and what it buys. We give a decomposition that separates confidence from leverage at the mechanism level, a proof
that no scalar of the bound distribution can recover leverage, the first control showing the signal survives
beyond geometry and beyond evolutionary conservation (the full feature set published hotspot predictors use), a
feature-class law that holds across four inverse-folding architectures, and steering of a frozen model that three
independent instruments confirm. Where prior work uses the direction, we characterise it, bound what it can and
cannot express, and show that a designer can read it and act on it.

A single principle runs through all of this: confidence is not competence, and it recurs at every level of the
modeling stack. An inverse-folding model's confidence (any scalar of its bound distribution) is blind to binding
by construction. A structure predictor's interface ipTM, confidence one level up, is blind to
binding-\emph{specificity}. It rewards a confidently-packed interface, which a mere foldability tilt achieves, so
a be-more-confident tilt beats the binding direction on ipTM while losing to it under models with an explicit
binding term. It takes a readout with such a term, the mixed derivative or a physics energy function, to see
binding at all. The competence always lives in the response to ablating the partner, never in the bound-state
confidence, whichever model does the reading.

There is a confound that no prior analysis controls, and it runs opposite to intuition. Hotspots are, on average,
\emph{more deeply buried} than other interface residues, and burial is precisely where inverse folding is
\emph{most} confident and accurate. An uncontrolled hotspot-vs-rest comparison therefore mixes a putative binding
effect with a large burial effect of the opposite sign, so the naive comparison \textbf{hides} any real deficit
rather than inventing one. Controlling burial is not a detail. It is the experiment.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A decomposition and a no-go theorem.} Confidence is the diagonal term of the inverse-folding
  log-likelihood and leverage is the mixed second derivative, and we prove leverage is not a function of the bound
  distribution. A flexible learner over the entire bound distribution recovers only about 37\% of it, so about
  63\% is irreducible from $P$ in both inverse-folding families.
\item \textbf{A feature-class law.} On natural complexes (SKEMPI) every scalar of the bound distribution
  (confidence, negentropy, the scalar KL) sits at or below a calibrated placebo floor, while the mixed derivative
  adds binding information beyond geometry, beyond evolutionary conservation, and beyond the one-pass log-odds,
  across four architectures.
\item \textbf{The published deficit is largely a burial confound.} Under a pre-registered matched-pair design the
  crystal-backbone hotspot deficit attenuates sharply across five architectures and under ProBID-Net's own
  released model.
\item \textbf{Steering a frozen model.} A $+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt on a frozen ProteinMPNN raises
  binding-favorability, confirmed by four inverse-folding judges, two structure predictors, and a physics energy
  function (FoldX), on crystal and on predicted backbones.
\item \textbf{Generality.} The blindness extends from binding to catalytic residues, and the recipe is the
  classifier-free-guidance direction for any conditional generative model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup and pre-registration}

\paragraph{Fixtures.} Our primary fixture is SKEMPI 2.0, from which we take single-mutation binding data and define
a hotspot as an alanine-scan $\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} > 1$ kcal/mol (strict variant $> 2$, ProBID-Net's
threshold), with a null set of $|\Delta\Delta G| < 0.25$. Hotspots are rare, 2.4\% of interface positions
(327/13{,}401, label entropy 0.115 nats), which sets the scale for the effect sizes below. Because SKEMPI
complexes are crystal structures of \emph{natural} complexes, we add two fixtures of different character.
Bennett-2023 de-novo designed binders carry site-saturation binding measurements over four targets, a true
design-regime test. AB-Bind gives antibody-antigen $\Delta\Delta G$ over 27 analysed complexes, a second
SKEMPI-class fixture with distinct biophysics.

\paragraph{The matched-pair protocol, and leakage.} The burial analysis rests on a within-complex optimal 1:1
matching of hotspots to null residues on relative complex SASA ($\pm 0.05$), secondary-structure class, and
neighbour count ($\pm 1$). All matching and all cross-validation are \emph{complex-level}: a complex is never
split across train and test, so a leak through near-identical interface structures cannot inflate a result.
Effects are aggregated by complex-clustered bootstrap, every seed is fixed (20260803) and reported with its
replicate count.

\paragraph{Statistical stance.} Headline claims are pre-registered, so their tests are confirmatory rather than
selected after seeing the data. The placebo floor calibrates the conditional-predictive-impact (CPI) estimator's
false-positive rate directly: a feature must clear the floor, not merely exceed zero. Every interval is a
complex-clustered bootstrap. The secondary and cross-architecture analyses are reported without a global
family-wise correction and are treated as descriptive support, not independent confirmatory tests.

\paragraph{Models.} Five inverse-folding architectures span the design space: ProteinMPNN (vanilla and soluble),
ESM-IF1 (a 142M-parameter GVP-transformer), PiFold (a one-shot GNN), MIF (masked inverse folding), and
ProBID-Net (a voxel CNN). A positive control gates every scoring path. These are inverse-folding \emph{sequence}
models, the stage our critique targets. Backbone generators such as RFdiffusion are the upstream stage and are
not steerable by $+\alpha \cdot L$ nor usable as a leverage judge, so they enter only through the
generated-backbone regime we test directly in Section 6.

\paragraph{What the evaluation answers.} Four questions organise the empirical sections. \textbf{(Q1)} Can any
scalar a designer reads off an inverse-folding model (recovery, confidence, entropy, a complex-vs-monomer KL)
locate binding hotspots beyond cheap geometry (Section 3)? \textbf{(Q2)} If not, where does the model's binding
knowledge live, and is it real beyond geometry, beyond conservation, and beyond the one-pass log-odds, on the
natural complexes the field benchmarks on (Section 4)? \textbf{(Q3)} Is that knowledge actionable, can it steer a
frozen model, and do independent models agree the result binds better (Section 4)? \textbf{(Q4)} Does it survive
the predicted backbones designers condition on, and is the published recovery deficit a real binding effect or a
burial confound (Sections 5 and 6)?

\section{Confidence is not competence}

The quantity a practitioner is most tempted to trust, the model's own confidence that the native residue belongs
at a position, is useless for locating hotspots. On SKEMPI interface positions, ProteinMPNN's per-residue
confidence attains an AUROC of 0.538 for hotspots, barely above chance, and for a fixed-budget triage (the top-3
interface positions per complex) it captures fewer hotspots than random selection (0.064 vs 0.084, overlapping
intervals, so a trend rather than a significant deficit). The point is that confidence buys nothing over random.

One might object that any single scalar can be rescued by combining it with structure. It cannot. We apply a CPI
test: cross-fit a model of hotspot-ness on cheap geometry (burial, neighbour count, $\Delta$SASA), then measure
how much predictive information confidence adds once its geometry-conditional information is destroyed by
permutation within geometry strata. The estimate is $0.000$ $[-0.0003, +0.0003]$: confidence is conditionally
independent of hotspot-ness given structure. By the same test, $\Delta$SASA adds real information ($+0.013$) and
the KL detector adds a token amount ($+0.002$).

This is not a quirk of one network. Across all five architectures, interface-hotspot confidence-AUROC lies in
$0.50$ to $0.54$ (ProteinMPNN 0.538, ESM-IF1 0.517, PiFold 0.499, MIF 0.509, ProBID-Net 0.536), each 0.15 to 0.19
below what trivial burial alone achieves. What predicts hotspots is free geometry: burial reaches 0.689,
$\Delta$SASA (the partner-contact area, computable without any network) 0.673, and a cheap-geometry combination
0.734. The learned alternative, a sequence-free KL detector between the complex- and monomer-conditioned
distributions, captures only a small signal (CPI $+0.002$, within-stratum AUROC 0.60, about six times smaller than
$\Delta$SASA). None of this is specific to SKEMPI: on AB-Bind the identical pattern holds (confidence-AUROC 0.560
at chance, adding $+0.008$ over full geometry).

\section{The Confidence--Leverage Decomposition: the model knows binding in the mixed derivative}

The results so far are corrective, and every quantity the field reads off these models (recovery, confidence, the
KL) is a scalar summary of the distribution. There is a specific reason they all fail at hotspots, and stating it
turns the corrections into a theorem.

\subsection{The decomposition and the no-go}

Write the model's per-position information as two terms of the inverse-folding log-likelihood. \textbf{Confidence}
is the \emph{diagonal}, a scalar functional of the single bound-conditioned distribution $p(\cdot \mid
X_{\text{complex}})$ (log $p(\text{native})$, negentropy), and estimates fold-stability constraint.
\textbf{Leverage} is the \emph{mixed second difference}, the per-substitution response to ablating the partner:
\[
L_i(a) = [\log p(a \mid X_{\text{complex}}) - \log p(\text{wt} \mid X_{\text{complex}})]
       - [\log p(a \mid X_{\text{monomer}}) - \log p(\text{wt} \mid X_{\text{monomer}})],
\]
which by the standard binding thermodynamic cycle estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ up to a single unknown
positive scale. This is exactly the classifier-free-guidance direction: a guided logit
$\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}}) + \alpha \cdot [\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}}) -
\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{monomer}})]$ has bracket equal to $L$ up to a reference shift the softmax absorbs.
The binding partner is the conditioner and confidence is the conditional marginal, so the recipe for reading an
un-trained quantity off a conditional generative model is to ablate the conditioner and take this mixed
derivative, not the marginal.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Proposition 1 (Confidence is blind to leverage).} Fix a model and an interface position $i$, with
$P_i = p(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}})$ and $Q_i = p(\cdot \mid X_{\text{monomer}})$. \textbf{(i, cycle)}
$L_i(a)$ estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}(i, \text{wt} \to a)$ up to one positive scale.
\textbf{(ii, non-identifiability)} $L$ is not a function of $P$: two positions with $P_i = P_j$ have identical
confidence $\varphi(P_i) = \varphi(P_j)$ for every functional $\varphi$, yet can differ in leverage, so any
$P$-only estimator carries an error floor $\inf_\varphi \mathbb{E}[(\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} - \varphi(P))^2]
\ge \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} \mid P)] > 0$. \textbf{(iii, readout, not model)}
$X_{\text{monomer}}$ is $X_{\text{complex}}$ with the partner deleted, a deterministic map, so $L$ is computable by
a second forward pass yet by (ii) is not a function of $P$. The binding term is in the weights, reachable only by
re-querying on $X_{\text{monomer}}$.
\end{quote}

The no-go is not vacuous, and we measure it. A flexible learner (the better of gradient boosting and random
forests, out-of-sample under complex-clustered cross-validation) trained on the full 20-vector $P$ recovers only
$R^2(L \mid P) = 0.37$ $[0.34, 0.40]$ for ProteinMPNN and $0.36$ $[0.34, 0.39]$ for ESM-IF1, so about 63\% of the
mixed derivative is irreducible from $P$ in both families (about 62\% even when wild-type identity is added). A
linear readout recovers less than half of that. Against ground truth the floor is starker: regressing experimental
$\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ directly on $P$ leaves 88\% irreducible for ProteinMPNN and 90\% for ESM-IF1.

\subsection{The feature-class law on natural complexes}

On SKEMPI, the mixed derivative adds binding information beyond cheap geometry where every scalar summary does
not (Table~\ref{tab:featureclass}, per interface position, CPI over burial, neighbours, and $\Delta$SASA). At the
mutation level the effect is large: Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G) = -0.30$ and CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry}) = +0.059$
$[+0.046, +0.073]$. The second pass earns its keep against the standard one-pass complex log-odds: controlling for
it and geometry, leverage still tracks $\Delta\Delta G$ (partial Spearman $-0.147$ $[-0.190, -0.108]$), and the
one-pass readout retains signal after controlling for leverage ($-0.094$), so neither pass subsumes the other.
The increment is not conservation in disguise: scoring each position with the field-standard ESM-2 masked-marginal
estimator (itself the stronger baseline, adding $+0.0064$ beyond geometry), leverage's contribution is
undiminished, CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry} + \text{conservation}) = +0.0059$ $[+0.0031, +0.0097]$. As a zero-shot
readout of a model never trained on binding, $L$ adds $+0.030$ interface AUROC on top of a \emph{supervised}
geometry-plus-substitution baseline fit on the labels, which the ground-truth floor predicts: with 88\% of
experimental $\Delta\Delta G$ irreducible from $P$, there is little for a supervised model to fit.

All comparators run under identical settings (temperature, decoding-order averaging, and sampling depth held
fixed), so the contrasts isolate the feature, not the protocol. The law replicates across four inverse-folding
architectures: ESM-IF1 (Spearman $-0.26$, CPI $+0.035$), PiFold ($-0.33$, $+0.050$), and MIF ($-0.27$, $+0.058$),
each surviving substitution, confidence, and scalar-KL controls, with confidence position-blind throughout. The
law is about a feature class, not a regime: on natural complexes scalar summaries reduce to geometry (confidence
exactly, KL nearly), while the mixed derivative does not.

\subsection{A dose law: fragility to backbone error}

The mixed derivative is read off a backbone, and it does not survive a large perturbation of one. Jittering the
crystal backbone to a target interface RMSD and re-scoring (the monomer inheriting the same jitter, so the
partner ablation stays clean), CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$ and Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G)$ hold through
sub-\AA{}ngstr\"om error, then collapse past a model-dependent knee (Table~\ref{tab:doselaw}). The decisive test is
whether real predicted backbones fall on the surviving or the collapsed part, and they fall on the surviving
part (Section 6): on OpenFold3 and AF2-multimer backbones for 140 shared complexes, CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$
is $+0.039$ $[+0.026, +0.050]$ and $+0.032$ $[+0.023, +0.041]$, 69 to 84\% of the matched crystal value. Because
the collapse is driven by the backbone the derivative is read from, the same fragility is a prediction every
method built on this mixed derivative inherits on predicted backbones, and the threshold is model-dependent
(about 1.0~\AA{} for ProteinMPNN, about 1.5~\AA{} for ESM-IF1), not a universal cliff.

\subsection{Steering a frozen model}

If $L$ is the guidance direction, the direct test is to guide with it. Biasing a frozen, off-the-shelf
ProteinMPNN's interface logits by $+\alpha \cdot L$ and sampling (K=64, 271 complexes, pre-registered grid) raises
the mean binding-leverage a \emph{different} model (ESM-IF1) assigns the sampled residues, monotonically, from
$-0.20$ at $\alpha=0$ to $+0.27$ at $\alpha=2$. A matched-magnitude random direction does the opposite, so the
paired $L - \text{random}$ gap reaches $+0.77$ ($P(>0)=1.0$ at every $\alpha>0$): it is the direction, not the
perturbation. Native interface recovery does not fall but slightly rises (0.276 to 0.297). Scoring with a
different model is what makes this non-trivial, and the steered sequences transfer to an independent structure
predictor: folded with AF2-multimer (120 complexes), the $L$-steered interfaces beat the random control on ipTM by
$+0.235$ $[+0.199, +0.272]$, interface-localized (global pTM rises about $2.9\times$ less). The effect is robust
across a matrix of independent readouts (Table~\ref{tab:steer}): a second structure predictor (Boltz-2), a second
steered model (ESM-IF1, judged by a third), and four anti-circular judges, with the trivially-circular self-judged
cells excluded.

A specificity control bounds the interpretation. A \emph{naive} tilt, the model's own log-odds direction rescaled
to $L$'s magnitude (be more confident, not bind better), recovers most of the ipTM gain and slightly exceeds $L$
there, because ipTM is itself a structure-level confidence and rewards a foldable, confident interface. This is
the same confidence-is-not-competence split one level up. The binding-specific increment survives where the
mechanism predicts, at the inverse-folding-judge level, where $L$ beats the confidence tilt (ESM-IF1 $+0.159$
$[+0.110, +0.207]$, MIF $+0.242$ $[+0.191, +0.292]$). The confidence tilt is a strong but binding-\emph{correlated}
baseline (it carries 64 to 77\% of $L - \text{random}$ at the judge level), not binding-\emph{specific}: a
confidently-packed interface is a prerequisite for binding, so the confidence direction rides along with binding
without targeting it. In the classifier-free-guidance framing, naive is the unguided marginal and $L$ is the
guidance term, so ``does $L$ beat naive'' is exactly ``does the guidance add over the marginal,'' which is why
naive, not random, is the control the framing demands.

\subsection{Physics adjudication (FoldX)}

To close the gap between inverse-folding judges and a real binding term, we ran FoldX (Guerois et al.\ 2002,
Schymkowitz et al.\ 2005, Delgado et al.\ 2019), a hand-fit physics energy function with an explicit binding term,
from a modality outside inverse folding (pre-registered, falsifier: $L - \text{naive}$ favorability $\le 0$). On
the 60-complex steering set, $L$ beats the confidence tilt on physics: a designer keeping the best of a few
samples gets $L - \text{naive} = +1.86$ kcal/mol $[+0.72, +3.02]$ (best-of-k, the design-relevant yield), and the
pre-registered mean-over-k primary is $+1.24$ $[+0.14, +2.33]$, both clearing zero (Table~\ref{tab:foldx}).
Because FoldX is physics, not an inverse-folding proxy, it sides with the judges ($L > \text{naive}$) against ipTM
(naive $\gtrsim L$), so the binding-specific advantage of $L$ is real, and the ipTM disagreement was the
foldability effect the frustration thesis predicts. As a detector, FoldX (fit to $\Delta\Delta G$) ranks better
than the zero-shot $L$ ($|0.43|$ vs $|0.30|$), and we do not claim otherwise. The narrower and more surprising
claim is that $L$ carries binding rank-signal beyond the fitted energy function: partial Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G
\mid \text{FoldX}) = -0.17$ $[-0.23, -0.12]$. The direction replicates on a second fixture (AB-Bind), same-signed
but underpowered at 22 crystal complexes.

\subsection{Predicted-backbone steering (the design regime)}

Every steering result so far tilts the model on a crystal backbone, but the staged-design regime has only a
predicted one. So we recomputed the leverage and applied the tilt on the predicted backbone (OpenFold3 and
AF2-multimer, 106 complexes), judging anti-circularly by an independent model's leverage also read off the
predicted structure (pre-registered, falsifier: judge-level $L - \text{random} \le 0$). The falsifier does not
fire and the effect is barely attenuated: judge-level $L - \text{random}$ is $+0.70$ to $+0.76$ across both
predicted backbones and both judges, essentially equal to the matched crystal control, and $L - \text{naive}$
stays positive ($+0.17$ to $+0.22$), so specificity is preserved. An independent structure predictor confirms it:
on 93 complexes, interface ipTM $L - \text{random}$ is $+0.18$ $[+0.14, +0.22]$. This is one dose law spanning both
uses of the direction: the design regime that preserves the readout preserves the knob.

\section{On crystal backbones, the hotspot gap is a burial artifact}

We now return to the published deficit and show, on crystal backbones, that it is a burial confound. The confound
is visible directly: as hotspot strength rises, both recovery and burial rise in lockstep (recovery 0.347 to
0.529, relative SASA 0.218 to 0.080). Under the matched-pair design the deficit attenuates to statistical
indistinguishability: the matched estimate is $-0.042$ $[-0.222, +0.129]$ and a higher-powered regression
estimator is $+0.059$ $[-0.051, +0.167]$. The matching does not control partner-contact area, so we check the
residual directly: adjusting for the $\Delta$SASA imbalance moves the deficit essentially nowhere ($-0.042$ to
$-0.028$). No architecture retains a hotspot deficit under matching. Most attenuate to zero, and two (MIF, PiFold)
flip to a hotspot advantage on the underpowered strict tier. The strongest form of the test uses ProBID-Net's own
released voxel-CNN: its port is faithful (interface recovery 0.472), its published deficit reproduces only in
comprehensively-scanned complexes, and it dissolves under confound-matching (matching residue type turns it
positive, burial gives $-0.038$, hydrophobicity $-0.051$, every interval spanning zero). ProBID-Net's deficit is a
residue-composition and burial confound, and we offer the matched-pair design as a reusable diagnostic protocol.

\section{The tax lives in the conditioning set}

If the deficit were a benchmark artifact it would vanish once burial is controlled. It does not. It reappears on
the predicted backbones designers condition on. On backbones from two architecturally-independent predictors
(OpenFold3 and AF2-multimer) a burial-matched deficit is present ($-0.191$ and $-0.233$, the crystal deficit is
about zero). The claim rests not on either marginal number but on their agreement: the two predictors' per-complex
deficits correlate at $\rho = 0.565$ $[0.40, 0.71]$, the same three complexes carry the effect under both, and the
agreement survives residualising on interface burial (partial $+0.529$). The effect tracks how a backbone is
non-native, not how far: on noised crystals at matched RMSD the deficit is absent, so it is a property of
independent reconstruction. And the binding signal a designer would read off these backbones survives them:
recomputed on the predicted structures, CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$ is $+0.039$ and $+0.032$ (pooled $+0.036$,
$P(>0)=1.0$), a 69 to 84\% retention of the crystal value, with the positive control reproducing the committed
leverage to $10^{-5}$. The confidence readout degrades into the deficit while the mixed derivative survives,
exactly where the decomposition says they must diverge.

\section{Ruling out competing mechanisms}

Two alternative explanations are separately measured and refuted. A \emph{sample-budget} effect (at low sampling
temperature a multi-residue hotspot constellation has astronomically small joint probability) is identical at
burial-matched control constellations (median difference 0.000, 95\% CI $[-1.67, +2.09]$ spanning zero): it is a
generic property of low-temperature sampling, not a hotspot tax, and no oversampling recovers it. A
\emph{commitment-ordering} effect (the autoregressive schedule paints hotspots into a corner) is inert on
ProteinMPNN (oracle-order difference-in-differences $-0.002$), and on MultiFlow the unmasking-order knob has only a
marginal effect (order-span 0.012, far below the 0.065 sample SD across seeds and reference structures). What
remains is the conditioning-set signal of Section 6.

\section{Related work and positioning}

The nearest prior methods use this operator, so we are exact about the boundary. The leverage operator $L$ is
BA-Cycle (Jiao et al., 2024), whose bound-versus-unbound double-difference rearranges to our mixed second
difference, and we credit it for the score. RedNet independently operationalises the same contrast as a
design-time decoder, verified in its released code, and we credit it too. What is not theirs and is ours is a
different object. \textbf{(i)} The decomposition identifies their score as the mixed derivative and confidence as
the diagonal, with the constructive proof that confidence, and every scalar of $P$, is blind to it, a question
neither poses. \textbf{(ii)} We give the first beyond-geometry and beyond-conservation control for an
inverse-folding binding signal, built on CPI (Watson \& Wright, 2021) with a conditional permutation test (Berrett
et al., 2018), which BA-Cycle and RedNet run in no form. \textbf{(iii)} We state the feature-class law.
\textbf{(iv)} We show frozen-model steering, where RedNet retrains a decoder and the direction is already a drop-in
tilt on a frozen model with native recovery preserved. \textbf{(v)} We validate anti-circularly across four judges,
two structure predictors, and a physics energy function, with a confidence-tilt specificity control no design
method has run. A frozen-vs-retrained head-to-head is not runnable (RedNet needs non-public packages and
access-restricted weights) and would be muddy, since the direction is shared, so the sharp comparisons are the ones
we report: $L$ vs random and $L$ vs the confidence tilt.

These add up to a beyond-X ladder (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}): the mixed derivative carries binding signal that
survives, in turn, geometry, substitution similarity, the one-pass log-odds, and a fitted physics energy function,
while every scalar of $P$ stays at the placebo floor at every rung. Read with the fractal motif of Section 1,
these are two axes of one dissociation: a scalar of anything is blind to binding-specificity, across controls and
across levels of the modeling stack, and the mixed derivative carries it. The sequence-free KL detector is a
learned frustratometer, a concept we credit to the classical frustration literature (Ferreiro et al.\ 2007,
Freiberger et al.\ 2019, Parra et al.), and our narrow point is only that this one scalar equals $\Delta$SASA,
which is why it does not beat physics, whereas the mixed derivative does. ProBID-Net reports the phenomenon as a
recovery deficit, and we correct the attribution: it is neither dynamics nor decoding but conditioning, and a
burial confound on the crystal benchmark. The most telling piece of practice is BindCraft, whose one-shot pipeline
hard-codes an interface freeze that forbids inverse folding at the interface, the field's implicit admission of our
thesis. Ranking by confidence is at chance there (which justifies the freeze), but adding the mixed derivative to
the standard geometric feature set raises hotspot AUROC over geometry alone (0.704) by a paired $+0.0125$ $[+0.0007, +0.0246]$,
so the corrected rule is to rank by geometry plus the mixed derivative, not by confidence. Independent corroboration
comes from Janusz et al. (2026), who report ``a very weak effect of antigen on the predictions'' in antigen-aware
antibody inverse folding, the same partner-insensitivity we quantify.

\section{Limitations}

We evaluate on three fixtures (SKEMPI, Bennett de-novo, AB-Bind), none a full generate-design-wet-lab loop, and we
are explicit about which half rests on what. The \emph{detection} claim is experiment-anchored: $L$ correlates
with experimental $\Delta\Delta G$ (Spearman $-0.30$) and adds beyond a fitted physics function (partial $-0.17$),
both against real measurements. The \emph{steering} claim's binding-specificity is anchored, out-of-family, by one
approximate-physics readout (FoldX, mean-over-k $L - \text{naive}$ $+1.24$, lower CI $+0.14$), so we scope it as
physics-supported pending a wet-lab test rather than experimentally proven, and do not overclaim it.

The primary claims rest on one natural fixture. This is structural to the field's data, not a gap in our search:
SKEMPI 2.0's curators already absorbed the non-redundant content of the other curated affinity databases, and the
subsequent resources are aggregators built around it. We mitigate with two further fixtures, four architectures, a
catalytic-site replication, and the cleanest non-overlapping alternative, ATLAS (TCR-pMHC), where the direction
replicates (Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G) = -0.22 / -0.32$ across the two models, bracketing SKEMPI's $-0.30$). The
direction thus generalizes across three interface classes (protein-protein $-0.30$, antibody-antigen $-0.18$,
TCR-pMHC $-0.22 / -0.32$), though only SKEMPI is powered for the geometry-controlled add-on. Tellingly, ATLAS is
the lowest-confidence interface class, so it tests the mixed derivative exactly where the scalar readout is
blindest, and the direction still points the right way. The blindness itself extends beyond binding, to catalytic
residues (M-CSA): inverse-folding confidence is blind (within-amino-acid-type AUROC 0.43 to 0.52) while a
sequence language model's conservation predicts them (0.77), a dissociation of $+0.288$ that survives composition,
burial, and chain-truncation controls.

Why an in-silico result is informative now. The detection half is validated against real $\Delta\Delta G$
measurements on three fixtures, so it is not in-silico in the sense that matters. The steering half is validated by
three instruments that do not share a failure mode (a second sequence model, two structure predictors, and a
physics energy function with an explicit binding term), which is the standard by which comparable design methods
are currently judged. A wet-lab loop is the right next step and is the one claim we scope as pending, not the
foundation the paper stands on.

Caveats specific to the decomposition. Orthogonal is not independent: confidence cannot express leverage, but the
two are weakly-to-moderately correlated and the correlation is model-dependent (Spearman(confidence, $|L|$) $=
+0.075$ for ProteinMPNN, $+0.30$ for ESM-IF1), so we claim blindness by construction, not statistical
independence. $L$ estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ only up to a temperature, and all headline readouts are
rank-based, but a single 2-parameter out-of-fold affine reads one unit of $L$ as about 0.42 kcal/mol (OOF RMSE
1.82, next to fitted FoldX's 1.79), a good ranker and a rough absolute predictor, kept out of every headline. The
decomposition replicates under a second architecture (ESM-IF1) on the 337 complexes both models cover.
