\begin{abstract}
Staged binder design generates a backbone and then inverse-folds a sequence, and the field reads the
model's \emph{confidence} to judge its interface choices. A prominent report quantifies a hotspot
deficit: recovery 0.334 at interface hotspots versus 0.472 elsewhere. That deficit is real but
misread: confidence and competence are two derivatives of one likelihood, and the field reads the wrong
one. Confidence is the \emph{diagonal}, any scalar of the bound-conditioned distribution $P$. Binding
\emph{leverage} is the \emph{mixed second derivative}, the response to ablating the partner. We prove leverage is
not a function of $P$, so every scalar the field reads is blind to binding \textbf{by construction}. On SKEMPI
natural complexes, confidence ranks hotspots at chance. The mixed derivative, a zero-shot readout, adds binding
signal beyond geometry, evolutionary conservation, and the one-pass log-odds (Spearman $-0.30$ with experimental
$\Delta\Delta G$, conditional predictive impact $+0.059$) across four inverse-folding architectures, and beyond a
fitted physics energy function (partial $-0.17$). It is also actionable: an $+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt on a frozen
ProteinMPNN raises binding-favorability against a matched random control, confirmed by an independent sequence
model, two structure predictors, and FoldX, on crystal and predicted backbones. The deficit is largely a
burial confound. The mixed derivative is the model's \textbf{classifier-free-guidance direction}: to read an
untrained quantity off a conditional generative model, ablate the conditioner. Code and analysis-ready data
reproduce every number and figure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The dominant paradigm for designing protein binders is staged: a generative model proposes a backbone, and an
inverse-folding model assigns a sequence by maximising $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{backbone})$. This
factorisation is convenient, but it omits the very quantity a binder exists to optimise. There is no
binding-energy term anywhere in $p(\text{sequence} \mid \text{backbone})$. The concern this raises is sharpest at
\emph{interface hotspots}: the small set of residues that contribute most of the binding free energy, and which
are frequently \emph{frustrated}, buried polar residues or strained rotamers that are locally unfavourable for
the monomer's fold but bought because they pay off in binding. If inverse folding optimises fold-compatibility,
the reasoning goes, it should systematically miss exactly these residues, and a hotspot should sit in the tail of
the model's distribution rather than at its mode. A prominent measurement appears to confirm this: ProBID-Net \citep{chen2024probidnet}
reports inverse-folding recovery of 0.334 at hotspots versus 0.472 elsewhere, and attributes the gap to dynamics.

This paper asks one question: when an inverse-folding model is used to build a binder, where, if anywhere, does
its knowledge of \emph{binding} live, and can a designer read it and act on it? The scope is deliberately narrow
and practical: structure-conditioned inverse-folding models (ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF1, and three others),
protein-protein interface binding, and readouts computable at design time. Our answer is that the binding signal
is real, but the field has been reading it from the wrong place. It has read \emph{scalar} summaries of the
model's output (recovery, confidence, a complex-vs-monomer KL) that are blind to binding \textbf{by
construction}, when it lives in a \textbf{mixed second derivative} that is cheap to compute and directly
actionable.

The consequence is immediate for practice, in three forms. As a \textbf{diagnostic}: inverse-folding confidence
is a geometry detector, not a binding score, so a pipeline that distrusts it at the interface (as the field's
leading one-shot designer does, by freezing the interface) is justified in doing so, and can now do better. As a
\textbf{training-free ranker}: the mixed derivative locates interface hotspots and adds on top of the exact
feature set published predictors already use, at no training cost. As a \textbf{drop-in steering knob}: a
$+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt biases a \emph{frozen, off-the-shelf} inverse-folding model toward higher-binding
interface residues with no retraining, confirmed by an independent sequence model, an independent structure
predictor, and a physics energy function. The recipe is not protein-specific: the mixed derivative is the model's
\textbf{classifier-free-guidance direction}, so the same move applies to any conditional generative model, which
places the result at a representation-learning venue rather than a purely methodological one.

There is a confound that no prior analysis controls, and it runs opposite to intuition. Hotspots are, on average,
\emph{more deeply buried} than other interface residues, and burial is precisely where inverse folding is
\emph{most} confident and accurate. An uncontrolled hotspot-vs-rest comparison therefore mixes a putative binding
effect with a large burial effect of the opposite sign, so the naive comparison \textbf{hides} any real deficit
rather than inventing one. Controlling burial is not a detail. It is the experiment.

We are precise about what is new, because the nearest prior methods already use this operator. The leverage
operator, a partner-ablated second forward pass, is the BA-Cycle operator of \citet{jiao2024bacycle}, and decoders
such as RedNet \citep{rednet2026} already tilt along this direction. Our contribution is not the operator but what
it means and what it buys: the decomposition and its no-go proof, the first control showing the signal survives
beyond geometry and beyond evolutionary conservation (the full feature set published hotspot predictors use), the
feature-class law across four architectures, and frozen-model steering that three independent instruments confirm.
We position against this prior work in full below.

A single principle runs through all of this, fractal across the modeling stack: confidence is not competence at
every level. An inverse-folding model's confidence is blind to binding by construction, and one level up a
structure predictor's interface ipTM is blind to binding-\emph{specificity}, rewarding a confidently-packed
interface that a foldability tilt achieves (Section 4). The competence always lives in the response to ablating the
partner, never in the bound-state confidence, whichever model does the reading.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A decomposition and a no-go theorem.} Confidence is the diagonal term of the inverse-folding
  log-likelihood, leverage is the mixed second derivative, and we prove leverage is not a function of the bound
  distribution: a flexible learner over all of $P$ recovers only about 37\%, so about 63\% is irreducible in both
  inverse-folding families.
\item \textbf{A feature-class law.} On natural complexes (SKEMPI) every scalar of the bound distribution
  (confidence, negentropy, the scalar KL) sits at or below a calibrated placebo floor, while the mixed derivative
  adds binding information beyond geometry, beyond evolutionary conservation, and beyond the one-pass log-odds,
  across four architectures.
\item \textbf{The published deficit is largely a burial confound.} Under a pre-registered matched-pair design the
  crystal-backbone hotspot deficit attenuates sharply across five architectures and under ProBID-Net's own
  released model.
\item \textbf{Steering a frozen model.} A $+\alpha \cdot L$ tilt on a frozen ProteinMPNN raises
  binding-favorability, confirmed by four inverse-folding judges, two structure predictors, and a physics energy
  function (FoldX), on crystal and on predicted backbones.
\item \textbf{Generality.} The blindness extends from binding to catalytic residues, and the recipe is the
  classifier-free-guidance direction for any conditional generative model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup and pre-registration}

\paragraph{Fixtures.} Our primary fixture is SKEMPI 2.0 \citep{jankauskaite2019skempi}, from which we take single-mutation binding data and define
a hotspot as an alanine-scan $\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} > 1$ kcal/mol (strict variant $> 2$, ProBID-Net's
threshold), with a null set of $|\Delta\Delta G| < 0.25$. Hotspots are rare, 2.4\% of interface positions
(327/13{,}401, label entropy 0.115 nats), which sets the scale for the effect sizes below. Because SKEMPI
complexes are crystal structures of \emph{natural} complexes, we add two fixtures of different character.
Bennett-2023 de-novo designed binders \citep{bennett2023denovo} carry site-saturation binding measurements over four targets, a true
design-regime test. AB-Bind \citep{sirin2016abbind} gives antibody-antigen $\Delta\Delta G$ over 27 analysed complexes, a second
SKEMPI-class fixture with distinct biophysics.

\paragraph{The matched-pair protocol, and leakage.} The burial analysis rests on a within-complex optimal 1:1
matching of hotspots to null residues on relative complex SASA ($\pm 0.05$), secondary-structure class, and
neighbour count ($\pm 1$). All matching and all cross-validation are \emph{complex-level}: a complex is never
split across train and test, so a leak through near-identical interface structures cannot inflate a result.
Effects are aggregated by complex-clustered bootstrap, every seed is fixed (20260803) and reported with its
replicate count.

\paragraph{Statistical stance.} Headline claims are pre-registered, so their tests are confirmatory rather than
selected after seeing the data. The placebo floor calibrates the conditional-predictive-impact (CPI) estimator's
false-positive rate directly: a feature must clear the floor, not merely exceed zero. Every interval is a
complex-clustered bootstrap. The secondary and cross-architecture analyses are reported without a global
family-wise correction and are treated as descriptive support, not independent confirmatory tests.

\paragraph{Models.} Five inverse-folding architectures span the design space: ProteinMPNN
\citep{dauparas2022proteinmpnn} (vanilla and soluble), ESM-IF1 \citep{hsu2022esmif} (a 142M-parameter
GVP-transformer), PiFold \citep{gao2023pifold} (a one-shot GNN), MIF \citep{yang2023mif} (masked inverse folding),
and ProBID-Net \citep{chen2024probidnet} (a voxel CNN). A positive control gates every scoring path. These are
inverse-folding \emph{sequence} models, the stage our critique targets. Backbone generators such as RFdiffusion
\citep{watson2023rfdiffusion} are the upstream stage, not steerable by $+\alpha \cdot L$, and enter only through the
generated-backbone regime of Section 6.

\paragraph{What the evaluation answers.} Four questions organise the empirical sections. \textbf{(Q1)} Can any
scalar a designer reads off an inverse-folding model (recovery, confidence, entropy, a complex-vs-monomer KL)
locate binding hotspots beyond cheap geometry (Section 3)? \textbf{(Q2)} If not, where does the binding knowledge
live, and is it real beyond geometry, conservation, and the one-pass log-odds? \textbf{(Q3)} Is it
actionable, can it steer a frozen model, and do independent models agree (both Section 4)? \textbf{(Q4)} Does it survive
predicted backbones, and is the published recovery deficit a real binding effect or a burial confound (Sections 5
and 6)?

\section{Confidence is not competence}

The quantity a practitioner is most tempted to trust, the model's own confidence that the native residue belongs
at a position, is useless for locating hotspots. On SKEMPI interface positions ProteinMPNN's per-residue
confidence attains an AUROC of 0.538 for hotspots, barely above chance, and in a fixed-budget triage (the top-3
interface positions per complex) it captures fewer hotspots than random (0.064 vs 0.084, overlapping intervals, a
trend rather than a significant deficit). Confidence buys nothing over random.

One might object that any single scalar can be rescued by combining it with structure. It cannot. We apply a CPI
test: cross-fit a model of hotspot-ness on cheap geometry (burial, neighbour count, $\Delta$SASA), then measure
how much predictive information confidence adds once its geometry-conditional information is destroyed by
permutation within geometry strata. The estimate is $0.000$ $[-0.0003, +0.0003]$: confidence is conditionally
independent of hotspot-ness given structure. By the same test, $\Delta$SASA adds real information ($+0.013$) and
the KL detector adds a token amount ($+0.002$).

This is not a quirk of one network. Across all five architectures interface-hotspot confidence-AUROC lies in
$0.50$ to $0.54$ (ProteinMPNN 0.538, ESM-IF1 0.517, PiFold 0.499, MIF 0.509, ProBID-Net 0.536), each 0.15 to 0.19
below what trivial burial alone achieves. What predicts hotspots is free geometry: burial 0.689, $\Delta$SASA (the
partner-contact area, computable without any network) 0.673, a cheap-geometry combination 0.734. The learned
sequence-free KL detector between the complex- and monomer-conditioned distributions captures only a small signal
(CPI $+0.002$, within-stratum AUROC 0.58, about six times smaller than $\Delta$SASA). None of this is
SKEMPI-specific: on AB-Bind the identical pattern holds (confidence-AUROC 0.560 at chance, adding $+0.008$ over
full geometry).

\section{The Confidence--Leverage Decomposition: the model knows binding in the mixed derivative}

The results so far are corrective, and every quantity the field reads off these models (recovery, confidence, the
KL) is a scalar summary of the distribution. There is a specific reason they all fail at hotspots, and stating it
turns the corrections into a theorem. The section builds one arc: the decomposition and its no-go, the
feature-class law that only the mixed derivative clears, the readout's fragility and reach, steering a frozen model
with it, and a physics and predicted-backbone adjudication.

\subsection{The decomposition and the no-go}

Write the model's per-position information as two terms of the inverse-folding log-likelihood. \textbf{Confidence}
is the \emph{diagonal}, a scalar functional of the single bound-conditioned distribution $p(\cdot \mid
X_{\text{complex}})$ (log $p(\text{native})$, negentropy), and estimates fold-stability constraint.
\textbf{Leverage} is the \emph{mixed second difference}, the per-substitution response to ablating the partner:
\[
L_i(a) = [\log p(a \mid X_{\text{complex}}) - \log p(\text{wt} \mid X_{\text{complex}})]
       - [\log p(a \mid X_{\text{monomer}}) - \log p(\text{wt} \mid X_{\text{monomer}})],
\]
which by the standard binding thermodynamic cycle estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ up to a single unknown
positive scale. This is exactly the classifier-free-guidance direction: a guided logit
$\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}}) + \alpha \cdot [\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}}) -
\text{logit}(\cdot \mid X_{\text{monomer}})]$ has bracket equal to $L$ up to a reference shift the softmax absorbs.
The binding partner is the conditioner and confidence is the conditional marginal, so the recipe for reading an
un-trained quantity off a conditional generative model is to ablate the conditioner and take this mixed
derivative, not the marginal.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Proposition 1 (Confidence is blind to leverage).} Fix a model and an interface position $i$, with
$P_i = p(\cdot \mid X_{\text{complex}})$ and $Q_i = p(\cdot \mid X_{\text{monomer}})$. \textbf{(i, cycle)}
$L_i(a)$ estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}(i, \text{wt} \to a)$ up to one positive scale.
\textbf{(ii, non-identifiability)} $L$ is not a function of $P$: two positions with $P_i = P_j$ have identical
confidence $\varphi(P_i) = \varphi(P_j)$ for every functional $\varphi$, yet can differ in leverage, so any
$P$-only estimator carries an error floor $\inf_\varphi \mathbb{E}[(\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} - \varphi(P))^2]
\ge \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} \mid P)] > 0$. \textbf{(iii, readout, not model)}
$X_{\text{monomer}}$ is $X_{\text{complex}}$ with the partner deleted, a deterministic map, so $L$ is computable by
a second forward pass yet by (ii) is not a function of $P$. The binding term is in the weights, reachable only by
re-querying on $X_{\text{monomer}}$.
\end{quote}

The no-go is not vacuous, and we measure it. A flexible learner (the better of gradient boosting and random
forests, out-of-sample under complex-clustered CV) trained on the full 20-vector $P$ recovers only $R^2(L \mid P) =
0.37$ $[0.34, 0.40]$ for ProteinMPNN and $0.36$ $[0.34, 0.39]$ for ESM-IF1, so about 63\% of the mixed derivative
is irreducible from $P$ in both families (62\% even with wild-type identity added), and a linear readout recovers
less than half of that. This same measurement rules out the benign alternative in which the second pass merely
rescales confidence (a temperature or mixture reparameterization), which would make $L$ a deterministic function of
$P$ and force $R^2(L \mid P)$ to one. Against ground truth the floor is starker: regressing experimental
$\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ directly on $P$ leaves 88\% irreducible for ProteinMPNN and 90\% for ESM-IF1.

\subsection{The feature-class law on natural complexes}

On SKEMPI, the mixed derivative adds binding information beyond cheap geometry where every scalar summary does
not (Table~\ref{tab:featureclass}, per interface position, CPI over burial, neighbours, and $\Delta$SASA). At the
mutation level the effect is large: Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G) = -0.30$ and CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry}) = +0.059$
$[+0.046, +0.073]$. The second pass earns its keep against the standard one-pass complex log-odds: controlling for
it and geometry, leverage still tracks $\Delta\Delta G$ (partial Spearman $-0.147$ $[-0.190, -0.108]$), and the
one-pass readout retains signal after controlling for leverage ($-0.094$), so neither pass subsumes the other.
The increment is not conservation in disguise. We score each position with the field-standard ESM-2
\citep{lin2023esm2} masked-marginal estimator, itself the stronger baseline (adding $+0.0064$ beyond geometry), and
leverage's contribution is undiminished: CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry} + \text{conservation}) = +0.0059$ $[+0.0031,
+0.0097]$. And as a
zero-shot readout of a model never trained on binding, $L$ adds $+0.030$ interface AUROC on top of a
\emph{supervised} geometry-plus-substitution baseline, which the ground-truth floor predicts (with 88\% of
experimental $\Delta\Delta G$ irreducible from $P$, there is little for a supervised model to fit).

All comparators run under identical settings, so the contrasts isolate the feature, not the protocol. The law
replicates across four architectures: ESM-IF1 (Spearman $-0.26$, CPI $+0.035$), PiFold ($-0.33$, $+0.050$), and MIF
($-0.27$, $+0.058$), each surviving substitution, confidence, and scalar-KL controls, with confidence
position-blind throughout. The law is about a feature class, not a regime: on natural complexes scalar summaries
reduce to geometry (confidence exactly, KL nearly), while the mixed derivative does not.

\subsection{A backbone-error dose law, and two further readouts}

The mixed derivative is read off a backbone and does not survive a large perturbation of one. Jittering the
crystal backbone to a target interface RMSD and re-scoring (the monomer inheriting the same jitter, so the partner
ablation stays clean), CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$ and Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G)$ hold through
sub-\AA{}ngstr\"om error, then collapse past a model-dependent knee (about 1.0~\AA{} for ProteinMPNN, 1.5~\AA{} for
ESM-IF1, Table~\ref{tab:doselaw}), not a universal cliff. The decisive test is which side real predicted backbones
fall on, and they fall on the surviving side (Section 6): on OpenFold3 \citep{ahdritz2024openfold} and AF2-multimer
\citep{jumper2021alphafold} backbones for 140 shared complexes, CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$ is $+0.039$ $[+0.026,
+0.050]$ and $+0.032$ $[+0.023, +0.041]$, 69 to 84\% of the matched crystal value. Because the collapse is driven
by the backbone the derivative is read from, every method built on this mixed derivative inherits the same
fragility on predicted backbones.

The signal also reaches the second mixed derivative and the de-novo design regime: the partner-ablated pairwise

\subsection{Steering a frozen model}

If $L$ is the guidance direction, the direct test is to guide with it. Biasing a frozen, off-the-shelf
ProteinMPNN's interface logits by $+\alpha \cdot L$ and sampling (K=64, 271 complexes, pre-registered grid) raises,
monotonically, the mean binding-leverage a \emph{different} model (ESM-IF1) assigns the sampled residues, from
$-0.20$ at $\alpha=0$ to $+0.27$ at $\alpha=2$, while a matched-magnitude random direction does the opposite (paired
$L - \text{random}$ reaches $+0.77$, $P(>0)=1.0$ at every $\alpha>0$): it is the direction, not the perturbation.
Native interface recovery does not fall but slightly rises (0.276 to 0.297). The steered sequences also transfer to
an independent structure predictor: folded with AF2-multimer (120 complexes), the $L$-steered interfaces beat the
random control on ipTM by $+0.235$ $[+0.199, +0.272]$, interface-localized (global pTM rises about $2.9\times$
less). The effect is robust across a matrix of independent readouts (Table~\ref{tab:steer}): a second structure
predictor (Boltz-2 \citep{passaro2025boltz2}), a second steered model (ESM-IF1, judged by a third), and four
anti-circular judges, self-judged cells excluded.

A specificity control bounds the interpretation. A \emph{naive} tilt, the model's own log-odds direction rescaled
to $L$'s magnitude (be more confident, not bind better), recovers most of the ipTM gain and slightly exceeds $L$
there, because ipTM is itself a structure-level confidence and rewards a foldable, confident interface, the same
confidence-is-not-competence split one level up. The binding-specific increment survives where the mechanism
predicts, at the inverse-folding-judge level, where $L$ beats the confidence tilt (ESM-IF1 $+0.159$ $[+0.110,
+0.207]$, MIF $+0.242$ $[+0.191, +0.292]$). The confidence tilt is thus a strong but binding-\emph{correlated}
baseline (64 to 77\% of $L - \text{random}$ at the judge level), not binding-\emph{specific}. In the
classifier-free-guidance framing naive is the unguided marginal and $L$ is the guidance term, so ``does $L$ beat
naive'' is exactly ``does the guidance add over the marginal,'' which is why naive, not random, is the control the
framing demands.

\subsection{Physics adjudication (FoldX)}

To close the gap between inverse-folding judges and a real binding term, we ran FoldX \citep{guerois2002foldx, schymkowitz2005foldx, delgado2019foldx5}, a hand-fit physics energy function with an explicit binding term,
from a modality outside inverse folding (pre-registered, falsifier: $L - \text{naive}$ favorability $\le 0$). On
the 60-complex steering set, $L$ beats the confidence tilt on physics: a designer keeping the best of a few
samples gets $L - \text{naive} = +1.86$ kcal/mol $[+0.72, +3.02]$ (best-of-k, the design-relevant yield), and the
pre-registered mean-over-k primary is $+1.24$ $[+0.14, +2.33]$, both clearing zero (Table~\ref{tab:foldx}).
Because FoldX is physics, not an inverse-folding proxy, it sides with the judges ($L > \text{naive}$) against ipTM
(naive $\gtrsim L$), so the binding-specific advantage of $L$ is real, and the ipTM disagreement was the
foldability effect the frustration thesis predicts. As a detector FoldX (fit to $\Delta\Delta G$) ranks better
than the zero-shot $L$ ($|0.43|$ vs $|0.30|$), which we do not dispute. The narrower and more surprising claim is
that $L$ carries binding rank-signal beyond the fitted energy function, partial Spearman$(L, \Delta\Delta G \mid
\text{FoldX}) = -0.17$ $[-0.23, -0.12]$. The direction replicates on AB-Bind, same-signed but underpowered at 22
crystal complexes.

\subsection{Predicted-backbone steering (the design regime)}

Every steering result so far tilts the model on a crystal backbone, but the staged-design regime has only a
predicted one. So we recomputed the leverage and applied the tilt on the predicted backbone (OpenFold3 and
AF2-multimer, 106 complexes), judging anti-circularly by an independent model's leverage also read off the
predicted structure (pre-registered, falsifier: judge-level $L - \text{random} \le 0$). The falsifier does not
fire and the effect is barely attenuated: judge-level $L - \text{random}$ is $+0.70$ to $+0.76$ across both
predicted backbones and both judges, essentially equal to the matched crystal control, and $L - \text{naive}$
stays positive ($+0.17$ to $+0.22$), so specificity is preserved. An independent structure predictor confirms it:
on 93 complexes, interface ipTM $L - \text{random}$ is $+0.18$ $[+0.14, +0.22]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:predsteer}). This
is one dose law spanning both uses of the direction: the design regime that preserves the readout preserves the
knob.

\section{On crystal backbones, the hotspot gap is a burial artifact}

We now return to the published deficit and show, on crystal backbones, that it is a burial confound. The confound
is visible directly: as hotspot strength rises, recovery and burial rise in lockstep (recovery 0.347 to 0.529,
relative SASA 0.218 to 0.080). Under the matched-pair design the deficit attenuates to statistical
indistinguishability (matched estimate $-0.042$ $[-0.222, +0.129]$, higher-powered regression $+0.059$ $[-0.051,
+0.167]$), and adjusting for the residual $\Delta$SASA imbalance moves it essentially nowhere ($-0.042$ to
$-0.028$). No architecture retains a hotspot deficit under matching. Most attenuate to zero, and two (MIF, PiFold)
flip to an advantage on the underpowered strict tier. The strongest form uses ProBID-Net's own released voxel-CNN:
the port is faithful (interface recovery 0.472), the published deficit reproduces only in comprehensively-scanned
complexes, and it dissolves under confound-matching (matching residue type turns it positive, burial $-0.038$,
hydrophobicity $-0.051$, every interval spanning zero). ProBID-Net's deficit is a residue-composition and burial
confound, and we offer the matched-pair design as a reusable diagnostic protocol.

\section{The tax lives in the conditioning set}

If the deficit were a benchmark artifact it would vanish once burial is controlled. It does not. It reappears on
the predicted backbones designers condition on. On backbones from two architecturally-independent predictors
(OpenFold3 and AF2-multimer) a burial-matched deficit is present ($-0.191$ and $-0.233$, the crystal deficit about
zero). The claim rests not on either number but on their agreement: the two predictors' per-complex deficits
correlate at $\rho = 0.565$ $[0.40, 0.71]$, the same three complexes carry the effect under both, and the agreement
survives residualising on interface burial (partial $+0.529$). The effect tracks how a backbone is non-native, not
how far: on noised crystals at matched RMSD the deficit is absent, so it is a property of independent
reconstruction. Yet the binding signal a designer reads off these backbones survives: recomputed on the predicted
structures, CPI$(L \mid \text{geometry})$ is $+0.039$ and $+0.032$ (pooled $+0.036$, $P(>0)=1.0$), a 69 to 84\%
retention of the crystal value. The confidence readout degrades into the deficit while the mixed derivative survives,
exactly where the decomposition says they must diverge. Two competing explanations for this residual, a
sample-budget effect and a commitment-ordering effect, are separately measured and refuted (Appendix C).

\section{Related work and positioning}

The nearest prior methods use this operator, so we are exact about the boundary. The leverage operator $L$ is
BA-Cycle \citep{jiao2024bacycle}, whose bound-versus-unbound double-difference rearranges to our mixed second
difference, and RedNet \citep{rednet2026} independently operationalises the same contrast as a design-time decoder,
verified in its released code. We credit both for the score. What is ours is a different object. \textbf{(i)} The
decomposition identifies their score as the mixed derivative and confidence as the diagonal, with the constructive
proof that every scalar of $P$ is blind to it, a question neither poses. \textbf{(ii)} We give the first
beyond-geometry and beyond-conservation control for an inverse-folding binding signal, built on CPI
\citep{watson2021cpi} with a conditional permutation test \citep{berrett2020cpt}, which neither runs. \textbf{(iii)}
We state the feature-class law. \textbf{(iv)} We show frozen-model steering: a drop-in tilt with native recovery
preserved, where RedNet instead retrains a decoder. \textbf{(v)} We validate anti-circularly across four judges,
two structure predictors, and a physics energy function, with a confidence-tilt specificity control no design
method has run. A frozen-vs-retrained head-to-head is not runnable (RedNet needs non-public packages and
access-restricted weights) and would be muddy since the direction is shared, so we report the sharp comparisons: $L$
vs random and $L$ vs the confidence tilt.

These add up to a beyond-X ladder (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}): the mixed derivative carries binding signal that
survives, in turn, geometry, substitution similarity, the one-pass log-odds, and a fitted physics energy function,
while every scalar of $P$ stays at the placebo floor at every rung. With the fractal motif of Section 1, these are
two axes of one dissociation: a scalar of anything is blind to binding-specificity, across controls and across
levels of the stack, and the mixed derivative carries it. The sequence-free KL detector is a learned
frustratometer, which we credit to the classical frustration literature \citep{ferreiro2007frustration,
freiberger2019frustration}, and our narrow point is that this one scalar equals $\Delta$SASA, which is why it does
not beat physics whereas the mixed derivative does. ProBID-Net reports the phenomenon as a recovery deficit, and we
correct the attribution: it is neither dynamics nor decoding but conditioning, and a burial confound on the crystal
benchmark. The most telling piece of practice is BindCraft \citep{pacesa2024bindcraft}, whose one-shot pipeline
hard-codes an interface freeze forbidding inverse folding at the interface, the field's implicit admission of our
thesis: ranking by confidence is at chance there (which justifies the freeze), but adding the mixed derivative to
the standard geometric feature set raises hotspot AUROC over geometry alone (0.704) by a paired $+0.0125$
$[+0.0007, +0.0246]$. Independent corroboration comes from \citet{janusz2026antigen}, who report ``a very weak
effect of antigen on the predictions'' in antigen-aware antibody inverse folding, the same partner-insensitivity we
quantify.

\section{Limitations}

We evaluate on three fixtures (SKEMPI, Bennett de-novo, AB-Bind), none a full generate-design-wet-lab loop, and are
explicit about which half rests on what. The \emph{detection} claim is experiment-anchored: $L$ correlates with
experimental $\Delta\Delta G$ (Spearman $-0.30$) and adds beyond a fitted physics function (partial $-0.17$), both
against real measurements. The \emph{steering} claim's binding-specificity is anchored out-of-family by one
approximate-physics readout (FoldX, mean-over-k $L - \text{naive}$ $+1.24$, lower CI $+0.14$), so we scope it as
physics-supported pending a wet-lab test, not experimentally proven.

The primary claims rest on one natural fixture. This is structural to the field's data, not a gap in our search:
SKEMPI 2.0's curators already absorbed the non-redundant content of the other curated affinity databases, and the
subsequent resources are aggregators built around it. We mitigate with two further fixtures, four architectures, a
catalytic-site replication, and the cleanest non-overlapping alternative, ATLAS \citep{borrman2017atlas}
(TCR-pMHC). The direction generalizes across three interface classes (protein-protein $-0.30$, antibody-antigen
$-0.18$, TCR-pMHC $-0.22 / -0.32$), though only SKEMPI is powered for the geometry-controlled add-on. Tellingly
ATLAS is the lowest-confidence class, so it tests the mixed derivative exactly where the scalar readout is
blindest, and the direction still points the right way. The blindness itself extends beyond binding, to catalytic
residues (M-CSA \citep{ribeiro2018mcsa}): inverse-folding confidence is blind (within-type AUROC 0.43 to 0.52)
while a sequence language model's conservation predicts them (0.77), a dissociation of $+0.288$ surviving
composition, burial, and chain-truncation controls.

\paragraph{Why an in-silico result is informative now.} The detection half is validated against real $\Delta\Delta G$ on three
fixtures, so it is not in-silico in the sense that matters. The steering half is validated by three instruments
that do not share a failure mode (a second sequence model, two structure predictors, and a physics energy function
with an explicit binding term), the standard by which comparable design methods are currently judged. A wet-lab
loop is the right next step, and is the one claim we scope as pending.

Caveats specific to the decomposition are detailed in Appendix C. The two that bound the headline claims:
orthogonal is not independent, so we claim confidence is blind to leverage \emph{by construction}, not that the two
are statistically independent (their correlation is weak-to-moderate and model-dependent). And $L$ estimates
$-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ only up to a temperature, so every headline readout is rank-based.

\section{Extended results and caveats}

\paragraph{The second mixed derivative and the de-novo regime.} The partner-ablated pairwise coupling tracks
measured binding epistasis with the cycle-predicted sign, Spearman$(C, g) = -0.14$ $[-0.23, -0.07]$, surviving
control for inter-residue distance (partial $-0.12$). The effect is modest and is a signal about coupling
magnitude more than sign, and partner ablation is what surfaces it: the un-ablated coupling is null. On de-novo
designs (Bennett-2023, site-saturation labels) even the scalar complex-conditioned distribution ranks
substitutions by binding (interface AUROC 0.615, above every substitution baseline), answers the stability
question better than the binding question (0.721 vs 0.615, the control a fold-trained model should pass), and adds
$+0.018$ beyond an all-atom rotamer-repacked occlusion baseline, reproduced by a non-parent scorer.

\paragraph{Ruling out competing mechanisms.} Two alternative explanations for the residual conditioning-set tax of
Section 6 are separately measured and refuted. A \emph{sample-budget} effect (at low sampling temperature a
multi-residue hotspot constellation has astronomically small joint probability) is identical at burial-matched
control constellations (median difference 0.000, 95\% CI $[-1.67, +2.09]$ spanning zero): it is a generic property
of low-temperature sampling, not a hotspot tax, and no oversampling recovers it. A \emph{commitment-ordering}
effect (the autoregressive schedule paints hotspots into a corner) is inert on ProteinMPNN (oracle-order
difference-in-differences $-0.002$), and on MultiFlow the unmasking-order knob has only a marginal effect
(order-span 0.012, far below the 0.065 sample SD across seeds and reference structures).

\paragraph{Caveats specific to the decomposition.} Orthogonal is not independent: confidence cannot express
leverage, but the two are weakly-to-moderately correlated and the correlation is model-dependent
(Spearman(confidence, $|L|$) $= +0.075$ for ProteinMPNN, $+0.30$ for ESM-IF1), so we claim blindness by
construction, not statistical independence. $L$ estimates $-\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ only up to a
temperature, and all headline readouts are rank-based, but a single 2-parameter out-of-fold affine reads one unit
of $L$ as about 0.42 kcal/mol (OOF RMSE 1.82, next to fitted FoldX's 1.79), a good ranker and a rough absolute
predictor, kept out of every headline. The decomposition replicates under a second architecture (ESM-IF1) on the
337 complexes both models cover.

\paragraph{Precise scope of Proposition 1.} The non-identifiability is a property of the model family, measured in
Section 4.1, not a universal law. Formally, $L$ equals a measurable function $\psi(P)$ almost surely if and only if
$Q = p(\cdot \mid X_{\text{monomer}})$ is $\sigma(P)$-measurable, which fails whenever the model map $X \mapsto
p(\cdot \mid X)$ is non-injective on a positive-probability set of inputs. It holds trivially, with $L$ carrying no
information beyond $P$, in the degenerate cases where partner ablation does not change the logits, or where the
second pass is a fixed reparameterization of the first (temperature or mixture), which the measured $R^2(L \mid P)
= 0.37$ excludes. The readout $L_i(a)$ is defined relative to the wild-type reference and is otherwise fixed only up
to that choice. Non-identifiability alone does not imply predictive gain, since conditioning on any feature weakly
reduces conditional variance. The gain is the separate, measured claim of Section 4.2, that
$\mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(\Delta\Delta G \mid P, L)] < \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(\Delta\Delta G \mid P)]$.
